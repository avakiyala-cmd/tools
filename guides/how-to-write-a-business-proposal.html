How to Write a Business Proposal That Wins Clients

A good proposal does three things: shows you understand the client's problem, lays out a clear plan to solve it, and makes the next step obvious. Here's how to write one that actually closes deals.

1. Open with their problem, not your bio

The first paragraph should prove you understand what the client needs. Reference their specific situation - the project they described, the deadline they mentioned, the challenge they're facing. No "we are a leading provider of..." - that goes at the end, if anywhere.

2. Define the scope of work

List 3-5 concrete deliverables. Use bullet points. Each deliverable should be specific enough that both parties know exactly when it's done. "Website redesign" is vague. "Redesign of 8 pages including homepage, about, services, and 5 product pages in Webflow with mobile-responsive layouts" is a deliverable.

3. Explain your approach

In 2-3 sentences, describe how you'll do the work. This isn't a full methodology document - it's enough to show you have a process and you've thought about their specific situation.

4. Set the timeline

Break the project into phases with realistic dates. Clients don't just want a deadline - they want to see that you've mapped the path from start to finish. Include key milestones where they'll review work.

5. State the investment

Use "investment" not "cost." State the number clearly. If you're flexible on budget, give a range. If you're not, give a fixed price. Never leave pricing vague - it kills deals faster than a high number does.

6. End with one clear next step

Don't give them three options. Give them one thing to do: "Reply to this email to confirm and I'll send the contract" or "Book a 15-minute kickoff call here." One action, one link.

Common mistakes that lose deals

  • Too long. A proposal is not a contract. One to two pages max.
  • Generic. If you could send the same proposal to a different client by changing the name, it's not specific enough.
  • Buried pricing. Don't make them scroll to page 4 to find out what it costs.
  • No deadline. Proposals without expiration dates sit in inboxes forever. Add "valid for 14 days."
  • Talking about yourself too much. The ratio should be 80% about them and their project, 20% about you.
